Geometric Growth with Environmental Stochasticity

Each population follows Nt+1 = λtNt, but λ varies independently from year to year. Run many replicate environmental histories to see how variance alone can create very different outcomes — including extinction. Population size is constant within each year and changes at year boundaries.

What to look for
Increasing environmental variance spreads the replicate trajectories apart. Even when the arithmetic mean λ is greater than 1, the geometric mean can fall toward or below 1, so the typical multiplicative trajectory may stagnate or decline. Rare strings of poor years can drive small populations below the extinction threshold.
